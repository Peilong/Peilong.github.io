    How a map app finds the fastest route

    The shortest-looking route is often not the fastest one, which is why map apps don’t just pick the path with the fewest turns. They use a method worked out by the computer scientist Edsger Dijkstra in 1956 and published in 1959.

    It works like this: start where you are, at 0 minutes. Then, again and again, take the closest place whose time isn’t final yet, mark its time as final, and see whether going through it gives any of its neighbors a faster time. When the destination’s time becomes final, it is guaranteed to be the fastest possible. Press Watch a computer solve it after a puzzle to see each step on the map.
